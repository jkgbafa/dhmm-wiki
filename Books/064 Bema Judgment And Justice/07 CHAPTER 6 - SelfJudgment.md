### CHAPTER 6\

Self-Judgment\
===================================

For if we would judge ourselves, we should not be judged. But when we are judged, we are chastened of the Lord, that we should not be condemned with the world.

1 Corinthians 11:31-32

It is important to judge yourself so that you will not be judged. What does this mean? Judging yourself is to take hard decisions against yourself. These hard decisions that you take against yourself will be humbling decisions that bring you low. The lower you bring yourself, the further you are from being brought low by someone else.

It is time to judge your self so that no one will ever have a chance to judge you.

How to Judge Yourself

1\. To judge yourself is to lead yourself properly. Leadership involves judgment. A leader is a judge.

The king by judgment establisheth the land: but he that receiveth gifts overthroweth it.

Proverbs 29:4

A leader is a decision taker. The kingdom is established through the judgments of a leader. There are three areas where a leader has to apply judgment. First of all, he has to apply judgment to the masses. That is the easiest part of the judgment of a leader. It is easy to see the wrong in other people and apply strict rules against them.

Secondly, he has to take judgment decisions against people that are close to him. This is very difficult. Whenever a leader has to take decisions that adversely affect those who are his friends and those whom he loves, he discovers the greatest challenge of his life.

One head of state who championed the independence of his country declared that the most difficult decision he ever took was when he had to take a decision against his best friend and associate, with whom he had launched the struggle and revolution.

The third level of judgment for every leader is when he has to take decisions against his own self. For instance, if he has to take decisions to curtail his privileges and benefits. Many leaders do not in any way curtail, limit or reduce the benefits that come to themselves. Because they fail to judge that certain things are unnecessarily excessive, they allow themselves to turn into opulent leaders.

2\. To judge yourself is to humble yourself without external input.

But when thou art bidden, go and sit down in the lowest room; that when he that bade thee cometh, he may say unto thee, Friend, go up higher: then shalt thou have worship in the presence of them that sit at meat with thee.

Luke 14:10

The second way to judge yourself is to humble yourself before you are compelled to do so. Judging yourself by humbling yourself starts from the way you see yourself.

It is important to see yourself as an average person. If you are a man, see yourself as an average man! If you are a girl, see yourself as an average girl! What is an average man like? An average man is someone who likes girls. An average man is someone who will not go through this life without fornication, adultery or immorality.

An average man is someone who has the tendency to be unfaithful. Since you are an average man, you must humble yourself and take whatever measures can be taken to prevent yourself from falling into the woes of an average man.

Do not consider certain things to be below you, far from you or impossible to you. You are an average man and you will do what average men do, unless you take extraordinary precautions against them. That is how to judge yourself!

Sometimes, Christian couples take a decision to live apart in different countries for long periods. An average man will not be able to stay faithful for two years without having sex. When you take a decision that would destroy an average man, you are not judging yourself fairly. Indeed, you are refusing to see yourself as an average man. Seeing yourself as an average man is the humble road to self-judgment. If you do not take this road to self-judgment, you will discover painfully that you are an average man and you will be judged for attempting to do things that only super humans do.

Judgment always brings about a lowering and a humiliation of sorts. If you decide to lower yourself, you will not be lowered by external forces. Many people live in hiding and in secrecy because they fear shame and lowering in the church.

When you are discovered to have been living in sin and deceiving people for many years, people are forced to place you in a lower position that may be far lower than you would have been if only you had voluntarily humbled yourself before others. When a great pretender is nabbed, he is treated like a rat or a snake that has been in hiding. There is a natural outburst of rejection and denunciation of rats and snakes when they are caught.

3\. To judge yourself is to punish yourself and take hard decisions that are painful to you whenever necessary.

And Nathan said to David, Thou art the man. Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I anointed thee king over Israel, and I delivered thee out of the hand of Saul; And I gave thee thy master’s house, and thy master’s wives into thy bosom, and gave thee the house of Israel and of Judah; AND IF THAT HAD BEEN TOO LITTLE, I WOULD MOREOVER HAVE GIVEN UNTO THEE SUCH AND SUCH THINGS. Wherefore hast thou despised the commandment of the LORD, to do evil in his sight? Thou hast killed Uriah the Hittite with the sword, and hast taken his wife to be thy wife, and hast slain him with the sword of the children of Ammon.

2 Samuel 12:7-9

There are kings and men of authority who are allowed to have as many women as they want. Some of these kings take advantage of this liberty and destroy people’s lives. King David was an example of someone who refused to apply judgment to himself. He was allowed to have many wives; and indeed he did!

Polygamy does not prevent fornication. Polygamy does not prevent adultery, per se. When David saw Bathsheba, he thought he could add her to his group of wives. But he should have judged himself, restrained himself and restricted himself to the women that he was legally allowed to have. If he had restricted himself and judged himself, he would not have entered into the trouble that he had in the latter part of his reign.

People in high up positions are expected to resign when something goes wrong. When something goes terribly wrong, you are expected to take a responsible decision and bow out. Your ability to restrict and to punish yourself is a sign of strong leadership with self-judgment.

When you wait for others to take that decision for you, it is a sign of pride and stubbornness. It is gross failure to not apply self-judgment when you should.
