### CHAPTER 17\

Understanding Personality Disorders\
---------------------------------------------------------

Personality disorders are conditions in which people differ significantly from others in the way they think, perceive, understand things and relate with others. A person with a personality disorder will have a different way of thinking, acting and believing which affects relationships with others and lasts over time.

Personality disorders are patterns of behaviour that differ significantly from what is expected. For instance, the way of thinking about oneself or the way of reacting emotionally, or the way of relating with other people, or the way of controlling your behaviour must differ significantly and remain so for a long time, for you to be classified as someone with a personality disorder.

Some of the common personality disorders are borderline personality disorder, anti-social personality disorder, avoidant personality disorder, histrionic personality disorder, obsessive compulsive personality disorder, paranoid personality disorder and narcissistic personality disorder.

1\. Anti-Social Personality Disorder is when a harmful person does not care about how others feel. Such people can become wicked criminals.

2\. Avoidant Personality Disorder has a pattern of extreme shyness and extreme sensitivity to criticism.

3\. Borderline Personality Disorder has a pattern of unstable and tumultuous relationships.

4\. Dependent Personality Disorder is when someone needs to be taken care of, unnecessarily. Such a person is usually clingy.

5\. Histrionic Personality Disorder is when a person seeks attention and is overly emotional.

6\. Narcissistic Personality Disorder is when someone is self-centred and arrogant and needs a lot of admiration. Such people become dictators and wicked leaders.

7\. Obsessive Compulsive Personality Disorder is when someone is pre-occupied with orderliness, perfection and control. It is what we would call a melancholy person.

8\. Paranoid Personality Disorder is when someone has a pattern of being suspicious and accusative.

9\. A Schizoid Personality Disorder is when someone is detached and expresses little emotion.

10\. Schizotypal Personality Disorder is when someone is eccentric, having odd beliefs and a peculiar behaviour.
