### CHAPTER 11\

King Saul: Bipolar Disorder\
-------------------------------------------------

Now the Spirit of the Lord had left Saul, and the Lord sent A TORMENTING SPIRIT THAT FILLED HIM WITH DEPRESSION AND FEAR.

Some of Saul’s servants said to him, “A tormenting spirit from God is troubling you.

Let us find a good musician to play the harp whenever the tormenting spirit troubles you. He will play soothing music, and you will soon be well again.”

“All right,” Saul said. “Find me someone who plays well, and bring him here.”

1 Samuel 16:14-17 (NLT)

There are people who swing between extremes. They may have a touch of bipolar disorder. There are people who were once seen as fiercely loyal but turned around and became fiercely disloyal. People who are both loyal and disloyal can be described as bipolar, swinging from the South Pole to the North Pole.

The life of Saul reveals the confusion of someone who swings between two poles. There are people who are neither here nor there. There are people who belong with you but also do not belong with you.

The life of Saul reveals the connection between evil spirits, madness and confusion. King Saul suffered from several well-known diagnoses. King Saul swung between two extremes and therefore reflected a possible bipolar disorder or a borderline personality disorder.

King Saul had a bipolar disorder because of his extreme mood swings and his uncontrollable emotions.

King Saul had a borderline personality disorder because of his erratic behaviour and sudden changes of emotion. There are many people who cannot make up their minds as to where they belong.

King Saul also suffered from paranoia because he was so afraid of little David, his harpist.

King Saul had delusions because he was so convinced that David wanted to harm him when David had no intentions of overthrowing him.

At that very moment the Spirit of GOD left Saul and in its place a black mood sent by GOD settled on him. He was terrified. Saul’s advisors said, “This awful tormenting depression from God is making your life miserable.

1 Samuel 16:14-15 (MSG)

The Scripture above declares that Saul was tormented with black moods that filled him with depression and fear. As Saul’s paranoia of David increased, he manifested bipolar symptoms.

Saul exhibited a change in his behaviour and personality after the evil spirit afflicted him. He swung from one extreme to the other as he related with young David. Saul’s personality seemed to change from being humble and merciful to being angry and hateful against David and Jonathan.

Saul was filled with depression and fear as he related with young David. He knew that his time was up because someone had replaced him in the realm of the spirit.

King Saul had strong symptoms of depression and mania. At times, he was accommodating and loving to David and at other times he was throwing a spear at him.

Saul also exhibited bipolar symptoms when he hunted David in the wilderness. When David refrained from killing Saul, they reconciled and returned home. David came to Saul’s court, only to be chased out again. On the second occasion, David crept into Saul’s camp in the night and cut a piece of his cloak. David confronted Saul the following day and begged Saul to explain why he was trying to kill him. Then Saul broke down and changed his mind in a fierce show of religiosity, calling on God and making emotional statements.

King Saul and the Two Extremes

1\. Saul employs David to play the harp and yet attacks him.

And it came to pass on the morrow, that the evil spirit from God came upon Saul, and he prophesied in the midst of the house: and David played with his hand, as at other times: and there was a javelin in Saul’s hand. And Saul cast the javelin; for he said, I will smite David even to the wall with it. And David avoided out of his presence twice.

1 Samuel 18:10-11

2\. Saul employs David to play the harp and yet attacks him again.

And the evil spirit from the LORD was upon Saul, as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand: and David played with his hand. And Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin; but he slipped away out of Saul’s presence, and he smote the javelin into the wall: and David fled, and escaped that night.

1 Samuel 19:9-10

3\. Saul attempts to kill David and then speaks in a loving reconciling way to David.

After whom is the king of Israel come out? after whom dost thou pursue? after a dead dog, after a flea. The LORD therefore be judge, and judge between me and thee, and see, and plead my cause, and deliver me out of thine hand. And it came to pass, when David had made an end of speaking these words unto Saul, that Saul said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And Saul lifted up his voice, and wept. And he said to David, Thou art more righteous than I: for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil. And thou hast shewed this day how that thou hast dealt well with me: forasmuch as when the LORD had delivered me into thine hand, thou killedst me not. For if a man find his enemy, will he let him go well away? wherefore the LORD reward thee good for that thou hast done unto me this day. And now, behold, I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand. Swear now therefore unto me by the LORD, that thou wilt not cut off my seed after me, and that thou wilt not destroy my name out of my father’s house. And David sware unto Saul. And Saul went home; but David and his men gat them up unto the hold.

1 Samuel 24:14-22

4\. Saul reconciles with David but still attempts to murder him.

And Saul knew David’s voice, and said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And David said, It is my voice, my lord, O king. And he said, Wherefore doth my lord thus pursue after his servant? for what have I done? or what evil is in mine hand? Now therefore, I pray thee, let my lord the king hear the words of his servant. If the LORD have stirred thee up against me, let him accept an offering: but if they be the children of men, cursed be they before the LORD; for they have driven me out this day from abiding in the inheritance of the LORD, saying, Go, serve other gods. Now therefore, let not my blood fall to the earth before the face of the LORD: for the king of Israel is come out to seek a flea, as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains. Then said Saul, I have sinned: return, my son David: for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day: behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly. And David answered and said, Behold the king’s spear! and let one of the young men come over and fetch it. The LORD render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness: for the LORD delivered thee into my hand to day, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the LORD’S anointed. And, behold, as thy life was much set by this day in mine eyes, so let my life be much set by in the eyes of the LORD, and let him deliver me out of all tribulation. Then Saul said to David, Blessed be thou, my son David: thou shalt both do great things, and also shalt still prevail. So David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place.

1 Samuel 26:17-25

During the first confrontation, David cried out, “Now understand and recognize that I am not guilty of wrongdoing or rebellion. I have not wronged you, but you are hunting me down to take my life,” to which Saul responded, “You have treated me well, but I have treated you badly. You have just told me of the good you did to me… When a man finds his enemy, does he let him get away unharmed? May the Lord reward you well for the way you treated me today.”

When David snuck into the camp the second time, he only stole Saul’s spear and water jug by the sleeping king’s head. The following day David questioned why Saul had changed his mind and hunted him down. Once again, Saul had changed completely.

I have sinned. Come back, David my son. Because you considered my life precious today, I will not try to harm you again. Surely I have acted like a fool and have erred greatly… you will do great things and surely triumph.

1 Samuel 26:21, 25 (NIV)
