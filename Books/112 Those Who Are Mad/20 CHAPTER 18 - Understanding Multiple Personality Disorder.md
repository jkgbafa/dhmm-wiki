### CHAPTER 18\

Understanding Multiple Personality Disorder\
-----------------------------------------------------------------

1\. Multiple Personality Disorder has actually been renamed as Dissociative Identity Disorder.

2\. It is a condition in which two or more distinct identifiable and recognizable personalities are seen in the same person.

3\. There is the existence of two or more distinct personalities within one individual.

4\. Only one distinct personality is evident at each point in time.

5\. Each personality has its own behavioural patterns and each personality takes control at a given time.

6\. The personality is unable to recall events and remember personal information in a way that is inconsistent with ordinary forgetting.

7\. These characteristics of a person with multiple personality disorder disrupt significantly and affect personal, family, social and other relationships.

8\. In some cultures, the experience of multiple personalities is called “Possession”.

Cases of Multiple Personality Disorder

Case 1:

A person with Multiple Personality Disorder could act like a great man of God, a powerful preacher, preaching and breathing holiness, fire and brimstone from heaven. And then when the second identity kicks in he could be acting like a drug dealer in a prostitutes’ den, enjoying multiple women in a smoke-filled cabin.

Case 2:

A person could act as an innocent untouched virgin, untainted with the life of the world in one setting and then act as a well-experienced sexually charged and experienced prostitute when she takes on her other identity.

Case 3:

A thirty-year old woman reported the presence of unusual experiences, dreams, since she was five years old. The patient reported that for twelve months, she was having episodes in which a second self, took charge of her actions. This lady would then speak with a male voice, become aggressive and require several people to contain her desire for destruction. After three months of follow up and accepting her unusual experiences and trances as normal, she had significant improvement.

Case 4:

Another example is of a fifty-five-year-old lady who presented at the hospital with a history of multiple personalities, which appear when she is under emotional stress or under the influence of a drug. The multiple personalities that appeared at different times in this lady were the personality of a seven-year-old child, a personality that would behave like a teenager, a personality that would behave like a male person, and the personality of her fifty-five-year-old person.

This lady reported that she had been constantly dominated by her alternative personalities and became aware of their existence when people around her informed her.

One of her personalities behaved like a seven-year-old child and would show the interest and choices of a seven-year-old, including becoming moody or arrogant. While in this state, she could hurt herself and have weeping spells if her wants were not met.

One of her personalities that behaved like a teenager would cause her to dress like a teenager and start smoking, drinking and fighting.

One of her personalities that behaved like a man made her change her voice and her behaviour. She would dress like a man and show attraction to females, including sexual behaviour.

One of her personalities was that of a fifty-five-year-old lady. That was her default personality.

She reported that stressful situations and substance abuse would aggravate the disintegration of her personality to bring out one of the personalities. Under the influence of stress and substances like marijuana, her personality would split into different personalities.
