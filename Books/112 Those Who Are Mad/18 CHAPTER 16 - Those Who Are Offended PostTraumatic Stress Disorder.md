### CHAPTER 16\

Those Who Are Offended: Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder\
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Woe unto the world because of offences! for it must needs be that offences come; but woe to that man by whom the offence cometh!

Matthew 18:7

It is important to keep a sweet spirit at all times. We are deep in a world, which is full of hurtful and painful things. You do not have to stay here for long before you get offended by someone.

Many people who attend weddings have little hope for the couple as they walk down the aisle. They are just waiting for the first phone call which announces the beginning of troubles, discouragement and despair in the relationship.

Jesus warned us that we would not be able to live in this world without getting offended. There are events that can hurt you so much that your life will be altered. There are hurtful things that people do that permanently damage relationships. These terrible hurts can give rise to the Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder.

Over the years, I have seen people respond differently to hurts and offences. I have also seen that different things offend different people. Some people cannot stand any form of public discussion about themselves. Some people do not like public discussions because the meetings expose and embarrass them.

Offences Turn People into Mad Men

And then shall many be offended, and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another.

Matthew 24:10

1\. Herod is offended and develops a mental disorder

But Herod the tetrarch, being reproved by him for Herodias his brother Philip’s wife, and for all the evils which Herod had done, Added yet this above all, that he shut up John in prison.

Luke 3:19-20

Herod, the king turned into a mad man because he was rebuked by God’s prophet. Herod went as far as attacking God’s prophet and imprisoning him because he was rebuked. Herod metamorphosed into a killer because he was rebuked about his marriage. Jesus said that John the Baptist was the greatest person to be born of a woman. Herod became the killer of the greatest person ever born of a woman! That is true madness!

Verily I say unto you, Among them that are born of women THERE HATH NOT RISEN A GREATER THAN JOHN THE BAPTIST: notwithstanding he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.

Matthew 11:11

It is not understood why some people are more prone to a Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder or to develop an unusual response to offence and hurt. Without treatment, the symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder are likely to worsen.

2\. Herod’s wife is offended and develops a Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

For Herod had laid hold on John, and bound him, and put him in prison for Herodias’ sake, his brother Philip’s wife. For John said unto him, It is not lawful for thee to have her. And when he would have put him to death, he feared the multitude, because they counted him as a prophet. But when Herod’s birthday was kept, the daughter of Herodias danced before them, and pleased Herod. Whereupon he promised with an oath to give her whatsoever she would ask. And she, being before instructed of her mother, said, Give me here John Baptist’s head in a charger.

Matthew 14:3-8

Herod’s wife was a regular woman having a regular affair. There are many women that have love affairs. However, when Herod’s wife was rebuked by John the Baptist, she was really offended and turned into a monstruous killer.

This over-reaction to a rebuke is a strong reminder of the reality of the Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder. A Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder can affect an individual until he becomes mentally ill. A soldier suffering from Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder can become depressed, suicidal and harmful to others. Herod’s wife became harmful to herself and to John the Baptist, just because he had spoken the truth to her.

Four Symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder in Herod’s Wife

First of all, Herod’s wife went through nightmares, flashbacks and fearful thoughts of John the Baptist’s strong preaching which highlighted her evil ways. She had recurrent, unwanted distressing memories of the church service in which her sin was mentioned. Each time Herod’s wife thought of John the Baptist, she relived the fear that came into her heart when the fact that she was a husband-thief was brought out.

Then, Herod’s wife went through what is called avoidance symptoms. She refused to think or even discuss what John the Baptist had said. She would now avoid John the Baptist at all costs or anything that reminded her of him.

The third set of symptoms affected Herod’s wife’s mood. She became negative, hopeless, detached, estranged, emotionally numb, guilty, depressed and anxious about the existence of this young preacher in town. This is probably when she asked her husband to arrest him.

The fourth set of symptoms of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder was reactionary and this is when Herod’s wife initiated the execution and cutting off of the head of John the Baptist. Aggression is a symptom of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder.

3\. The Pharisees are offended and become murderers

Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. Ye blind guides, which strain at a gnat, and swallow a camel. Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye make clean the outside of the cup and of the platter, but within they are full of extortion and excess.

Matthew 23:23-25

An offence or a rebuke at a meeting is not supposed to turn you into something that has deviated grotesquely from the normal expected form. Pharisees were religious men. Pharisees were supposed to be men of love, men of kindness and men with a good heart. As Jesus preached the truth to them, they were offended and reacted very badly. They became men of treachery, men of hatred, full of envy and murder. They ended up murdering Jesus Christ, the holy Son of God because they were offended. Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder is an abnormal response to correction and rebukes.

It is normal to feel disturbed and afraid after a traumatic experience. This is your body’s way of protecting itself from further harm. With time, most people recover from the effects of trauma and offence. But people with Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder do not feel better and do not recover from the hurts and offence. They feel stressed, frightened and affected long after the event is over! Watch out for those who are reacting continuously and endlessly to offences. Many of such people are going mad!
