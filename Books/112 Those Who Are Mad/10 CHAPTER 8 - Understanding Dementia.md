### CHAPTER 8\

Understanding Dementia\
--------------------------------------------

Dementia is a general term for the loss of memory, the loss of language, the loss of the ability to solve problems and the loss of the ability to think.

Memory often changes as people grow older. Some people notice changes in their memory before anyone else does. Friends and family are often the first to see changes in memory, behaviour or abilities.

Your memory changes as you grow older but it is not supposed to disrupt your daily life. When the loss of memory disrupts your daily life, then there is a problem. Memory loss that disrupts daily life is not a normal part of aging.

Dementia occurs when the loss of memory leads to troubling behaviour problems and communication difficulties.

How can you diagnose dementia in someone? You can diagnose dementia if you make some of the following observations in a person. People with any of these signs may be developing dementia.

Twenty Signs of Dementia

1\. A person with dementia may be unable to recall information about himself, like his address or telephone number, the high school or university he attended.

2\. A person with dementia may forget his personal history.

3\. A person with dementia may forget important events that have taken place in his life.

4\. A person with dementia may forget things that he has learnt very well, like things he does at work.

5\. A person with dementia may forget things, even after he has been reminded of them.

6\. A person with dementia may not recognize family members.

7\. A person with dementia may forget relationships that he has.

8\. A person with dementia may call family members by other names.

9\. A person with dementia may become confused about the location of his home or the passage of time.

10\. A person with dementia may forget the use of common items, such as the use of a pen or the use of cutlery.

11\. A person with dementia can become tired and unable to concentrate. The tiredness can cause people with dementia not to eat. A lack of concentration and coordination can make it difficult for them to eat.

12\. Dementia is characterized by increasing confusion about where they are or even what day it is.

13\. Dementia is characterized by poor judgment and may require help in choosing proper clothing for the season or in controlling their bladder and bowels.

14\. Dementia is characterized by significant behaviour changes.

15\. A person with dementia shows an increased tendency to wander and to become lost.

16\. A person with dementia may be moody or withdrawn, especially in socially or mentally challenging situations.

17\. A person with dementia may demonstrate personality and behavioural changes, becoming more irritable, upset or emotional.

18\. A person with dementia may develop suspicions and delusions.

19\. A person with dementia may eventually be confined to a bed or a chair.

20\. A person with dementia may die because damaged brain and nerve cells can lead to pneumonia, strokes, falls, infections or malnutrition that can be fatal. Alzheimer’s disease is the most common manifestation of dementia among older adults. It is caused by changes in the brain, including abnormal build-ups of proteins known as amyloid plaques and tau tangles. The average life expectancy for Alzheimer’s disease is around eight to ten years.
