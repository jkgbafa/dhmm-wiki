A WARNING FROM HISTORY

Fighting with an encrypted and coded language was a major part of the Second World War. During the Second World War, the Atlantic Ocean became a very important route because British and American ships travelled across it to bring supplies and goods to Britain. Without travelling across the Atlantic Ocean, Britain could not survive the war. Everything that came to Britain from anywhere somehow had to come through the Atlantic Ocean.

The Germans developed submarines which they called U-boats which lined up in the Atlantic Ocean and torpedoed and sunk all ships going to Britain with supplies.

The submarines of the Germans were a terrible menace to the British during the war. During the first seven months of the war, the U-boats (German submarines) sank 200 ships.

The Famous Submarine U-boat Encrypted Code

The German submarines had to communicate with each other under the water so they could congregate where the British ships were, torpedo them and sink them. Unfortunately for the Germans, these radio messages from the submarines could be intercepted by the British ships and they could localise where the German submarines were and bomb them out of the water. The German submarines therefore developed a special encrypted language that no one could understand or interpret. This helped them to escape from the British under the water.

The Germans had what they called an enigma machine which could send highly coded messages that the British could not understand no matter what they did. If only the British could understand the radio messages, they would know where the U-boats were and be able to attack them!

The Germans used the Morse code that was encrypted by the enigma machine. Every letter that was typed into the enigma machine would cause another letter to come up. There were millions of different settings and it was simply impossible for the British to understand their enemy’s messages or plans.

The secret and encrypted language of communication used by the German submarines was so successful that by the time the war was over they had sunk over 4,000 ships and killed over 29,000 seamen. This encrypted message of communication was very successful and was the great secret of the German submarines.

The Day the Encrypted Code of the Germans Was Discovered

Britain only began to win the war against Germany when they decoded the secret unintelligible and encrypted messages of the German submarines.

One day, one of the German submarines (code-named U-110) was damaged and surfaced on the ocean. U-110 had survived an attack but was seriously damaged. The captain of the submarine, Kapitänleutnant Lemp announced “Last stop, everybody out”, meaning “Abandon ship”.

The submarine crew came under fire from the British when they came up onto the U-boat’s deck. As the crew attempted to swim away, Kapitan Lemp realised that U-110 was not sinking and attempted to swim back to it to destroy the secret material but was shot by a British soldier and was never seen again.

A British naval boarding party, led by Sub-Lieutenant David Balme, got onto the U-110 submarine and stripped it of everything portable, including her Kurzsignale (Short Signal Book) and the long sought-after enigma machine. The British had now captured a submarine that had their enigma machines on board.

With the help of the captured enigma machine, the British were able to figure out the codes of the German U-Boats and from then on, intercepted all the messages of the submarines.

How the Code Won the War

After they took the enigma machine and cracked the code, the British were able to divert all their convoys away from the submarines and no longer experienced terrible losses of ships and lives.

This immediately turned the tide of the war against Germany and the Germans never understood why things were changing so much. After a while, the Germans must have realized that their secret encrypted messages were being read by the British.
