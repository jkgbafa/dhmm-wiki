The Power of a Bomb

Bombs have been used as weapons for years because they cause shock waves that lead to the rupturing of the eardrums, people being thrown through the air, and dismembering of bodies. Bombs also cause the release of extreme heat that reaches temperatures of 2,500 degrees Celsius. The heat from bombs causes extreme burns. Fragmentation of exploding bombs also occurs, together with the acceleration of the shattered pieces and physical particles of the bomb into the air. Small metal pieces of the bomb which kill, blind and harm people can be thrown into the air at supersonic and hypersonic speeds.

Bombs Harm the Enemy Greatly

Because of the devastating effects of bombs and explosions, the releasing of a bomb is one of the important acts of war. The most powerful non-nuclear bomb ever created can destroy an area equivalent to nine city blocks. A normal bomb such as those used in the Second World War produces the effect of 10 tonnes of TNT. The Hiroshima nuclear bomb that flattened the city, contained the power of 13,000 tonnes or 13 kilotons. If 13,000 tonnes could flatten Hiroshima city, then what kind of power would be released with a 13,000,000 or even 50,000,000 tonnes nuclear bomb?

The world’s largest ever tested nuclear bomb now gives off a yield of 58 mega tonnes, which is 58,000,000 tonnes of explosive power. As you can see, the size of the nuclear bomb has greatly increased from 13,000 to 58,000,000 tonnes. There are ballistic missiles that can carry these nuclear weapons and release their deadly explosions on their targets.
