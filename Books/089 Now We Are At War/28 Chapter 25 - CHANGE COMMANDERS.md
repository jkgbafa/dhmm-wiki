It is important to change commanders and leaders who are failing to bring the victory. The key to monumental improvements is to change the commanders. God is a God who changes people. Be a follower of God! You must change people who you have appointed when they are not performing well. You must transfer people who are not able to bring church growth. It is also important to tell people why they are being transferred.

I remember one pastor who complained that he was being transferred from place to place. In actual fact, he had been moved because he was not doing well. If you are part of an army, you will be subject to a change in the commanders. Even God changes his commanders. Anyone who fails to make a change in the commanders is setting himself up for more defeat.

Do not be afraid to dismiss people. Do not be afraid to institute major changes. Do not be afraid to make new appointments. A good general is often forced to change commanders in the midst of the war. Do not be afraid to try new commanders! A change in commanders can help you win the war. Your churches will change when you change commanders.

A LESSON FROM HISTORY

There are two names that are famous in the military campaigns of the Second World War - General Montgomery and General Patton. Both of these famous men were replacements and came into the war as replacements and new commanders. Winston Churchill and General Eisenhower were not afraid to try out these new commanders on the field of battle.

General Montgomery

General Montgomery was brought in to become the commander in North Africa. Why was General Montgomery brought in? Why was the previous commander changed? Why did the British Army change their commander in North Africa?

The commander before General Montgomery was called Auchinleck. This man had a strained relationship with the Prime Minister, Winston Churchill. Prime Minister Winston Churchill was intensely frustrated by the slow progress and repeated setbacks in North Africa. He constantly pressed Auchinleck for offensive action and a decisive victory.

Auchinleck, while a capable strategist, often resisted Churchill’s impatient demands, believing the army needed more time for training, reinforcement and proper preparation before launching major offensives. This led to a very poor relationship between the two, with Churchill feeling that Auchinleck lacked the necessary aggression. Auchinleck was seen by many as being too defensive in his approach.

Churchill wanted a commander who would seize the initiative and launch a strong counter-offensive to defeat the Germans decisively. This is why Prime Minister Winston Churchill opted to change his commander. The change in the commander brought about the change in the war, with the British eventually winning the war in North Africa.

Initially, the change in the North African campaign was to bring in General William Gott. Unfortunately, General Gott was killed when his plane was shot down while enroute to take up the position as the new commander.

General Bernard Montgomery was then brought in to replace General William Gott as the commander of the Eighth Army.

17Field Marshal Bernard Law Montgomery ended up becoming one of the most prominent and successful British commanders of the Second World War (1939-45). The name General Montgomery is well known. He brought major victories to the allied armies. Affectionately known as ‘Monty’, he commanded the Allies in North Africa and in the subsequent invasions of Italy and Normandy.17

After the British Army had suffered many defeats and was in retreat against German General Erwin Rommel’s forces, Montgomery was appointed as the new commander with the hope that he would be capable of restoring confidence and turning the tide. Montgomery succeeded and led the eighth army to a decisive victory at the Battle of El Alamein.

General Patton

General Patton was brought in as commander because his predecessor, General Lloyd Fredendall, had a more cautious and methodical style contrasting with General Patton’s highly aggressive breakout style. Patton, with his aggressive “Blitzkrieg” style, was later able to demonstrate a much faster manoeuvre warfare once he was unleashed.

General George Patton of the US army was brought in to replace General Lloyd Fredendall. General Fredendall was responsible for the disastrous massacre of 3000 American troops in two days in the Atlas Mountains. They were massacred by the German General Rommel. General Fredendall was fired for this terrible failure and replaced with a little-known officer called Lieutenant General George Patton.

Upon taking command, General Patton set about to transform the demoralized and disorganised army into an effective fighting force. His actions were swift, decisive and characteristic of his leadership style. He restored discipline and morale. He imposed strict dress codes and disciplinary rules. He cracked down on sloppiness and demanded obedience and aggressive action. He implemented rigorous training.

Just ten days after taking command, Patton led the army in the Battle of El Guettar. Patton’s presence and aggressive leadership were instrumental in this successful turnaround.
