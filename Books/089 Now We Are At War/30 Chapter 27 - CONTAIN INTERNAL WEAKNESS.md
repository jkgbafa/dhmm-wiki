### Chapter 27\

CONTAIN INTERNAL WEAKNESS\
-----------------------------------------------------

\

##### APART FROM SUCH EXTERNAL THINGS, there is the daily pressure on me of concern for all the churches.

##### 2 Corinthians 11:28 (NASB)

\

Paul had external pressures and crises. But apart from those external pressures, he had internal weaknesses and pressures that he had to contain.

...Most gladly, therefore, I will rather boast about my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me. Therefore I delight in weaknesses, in insults, in distresses, in persecutions, in difficulties, in behalf of Christ; for when I am weak, then I am strong.

2 Corinthians 12:9-10 (NASB)

There are insults, embarrassments, reproaches, shameful things and distressing things that multiply the stresses and tensions of ministry. There are times when the internal pressure of ministry is greater than anything external.

Internal weakness and human failure cause conflict, shame, weakness and embarrassment. Internal weakness and failings can weaken and embarrass the whole church. Many good generals are distracted more by their internal weaknesses than anything else.

We who are strong ought to bear with the failings of the weak and not to please ourselves.

Romans 15:1 (NIV)

Your ability to contain embarrassments, internal weaknesses and failings is a reflection of the strength of the church. Weak nations can hardly look after the normal people in society, let alone the weak. In weaker nations, people with weaknesses and embarrassing conditions are set aside and neglected. The blind, the poor, the handicapped and the prisoners of wealthy nations are much better looked after than those in poorer nations.

Containing Internal Weakness

1\. Achan: A cause of embarrassment

And Joshua said unto Achan, My son, give, I pray thee, glory to the Lord God of Israel, and make confession unto him; and tell me now what thou hast done; hide it not from me.

Joshua 7:19

Achan was a source of internal weakness and embarrassment to Israel. Achan represents weakness, treachery and deception. Such people take away the strength of the whole church.

An Achan is somebody whose presence takes away the victory. Achan was an internal failing and weakness of Israel and had to be handled with care so as to please God. How do you deal with an embarrassing Achan? Every Achan must be properly disciplined and judged. It may even cost him his life and ministry.

2\. Peter: A source of internal weakness

Then Peter took him, and began to rebuke him, saying, Be it far from thee, Lord: this shall not be unto thee. But he turned, and said unto Peter, Get thee behind me, Satan: thou art an offence unto me: for thou savourest not the things that be of God, but those that be of men.

Matthew 16:22-23

Peter attempted to rebuke Jesus. Peter wanted to take over and direct the ministry for a few minutes. Peter was sure he knew what to do next.

Peter was the appointed head of the church who embarrassed the Lord. How could you rebuke the son of God just because you received an appointment to be the head of the church? What an embarrassing and mistaken appointment that seemed to be!

Peter clearly appeared to be the wrong man for the job. Weaknesses in the leadership can lead to difficulty for the whole team. Many army commanders bungle up their jobs. However, it is the duty of the army to handle things carefully and manage internal weakness.

What do you do with people who seem out of place? Peter was given a very stern rebuke and even called satan! I am sure he never forgot it.

3\. Judas: A source of shame

Judas was an embarrassment to Jesus’ ministry. He collaborated with the murderous Pharisees and made them have a much lower impression of Jesus and His disciples.

Judas was a thief! How could Jesus have put a thief in charge of money? Perhaps all the other disciples were also thieves. It looked like a gross mistake to put Judas in charge of the money. Perhaps people in town knew that Jesus’ financial secretary was a thief. He probably had deals with people in town.

Jesus allowed Judas to judge himself and execute himself. He was an embarrassment to the team and the end came in a fitting and appropriate way and by his own hand.

This he said, not that he cared for the poor; but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and bare what was put therein.

John 12:6

Jesus allowed Judas to be overcome with remorse and to take his own life. Jesus could have advised Judas against suicide, but He allowed Judas to implement his own judgment. Jesus was never seen to have lifted His hand against His own wicked betrayer. Thus, Judas was eliminated by his own hand. The elimination of internal embarrassing weakness must be done with decorum and must have all the hallmarks of love, decency, respect and mercy.

A Warning from History

Marcus Aurelius was one of the five good and famous emperors of the Roman Empire. His wife was Empress Faustina. While Marcus Aurelius walked a path of decency, his wife, Faustina the Younger, was controversial, to say the least. The Roman elite whispered tales of her cruel and scandalous behaviour, yet the philosopher-king humbly accepted her as she was and contained the weaknesses and embarrassment that she brought to Rome.

On a certain occasion, Faustina, saw some gladiators pass by and was inflamed with love for one of them. Afterwards, she confessed her passion to her husband. When Marcus Aurelius reported this affair to his advisors, it was suggested that Faustina should bathe in the gladiator’s blood to restore her to her husband. Aside gladiators, Faustina is believed to have slept with other sailors and soldiers. One lasting legacy of these affairs was the rumours about her son, Emperor Commodus, who people said was the son of Faustina’s gladiator lover.

Though never confirmed, many believe that she had an affair with a gladiator because Commodus, her son, actually acted as a gladiator during his reign as emperor. There were also rumours about Faustina ordering deaths, including poisonings and executions, which made many believe she was evil.

Not once did Marcus Aurelius publicly reproach Faustina during her lifetime. Instead, he only spoke of her with unwavering respect.

In addition to all this, Faustina was alleged to have been involved in an affair with Avidius Cassius who had rebelled against her husband the emperor. She believed her husband, Marcus Aurelius, was dying and she wanted a strong successor to protect her and her young son Commodus who was thirteen years old.

The revolt of Avidius Cassius failed after three months when he was killed and it is said that Faustina committed suicide because of this turn of events.

Marcus Aurelius only spoke of his wife with deep love, respect and admiration and never hinted at infidelity. He buried Faustina the Younger at the Mausoleum of Hadrian in Rome and deified her name.

You must learn to deal with embarrassment and weakness in a way that would not make you look like a merciless leader. Your elimination of your internal and embarrassing weaknesses must be done with decorum and must have all the hallmarks of love, decency and respect.
