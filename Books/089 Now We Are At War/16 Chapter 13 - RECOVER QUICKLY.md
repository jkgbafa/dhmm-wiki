A Lesson of Recovery

In the secular world, they always attempt to recover from every war and crisis. Great efforts are put in place to recover from the devastation of a war. It’s important to put measures in place to recover from any attack that has happened in your life. Let us see how worldly people make efforts to recover, rather than leaving recovery to chance and happenstance.

The Devastation of World War ll

The effect of World War II was the ultimate and profound destruction everywhere. Millions of European citizens were killed or seriously wounded in World War II and the Holocaust.

Many cities like London, Dresden, Berlin, Cologne, Liverpool, Birmingham and Hamburg were partly or wholly destroyed by the war.

Some regions of Europe experienced famine because agriculture and food production were disrupted by the fighting.

Railways, electric utilities, port facilities, roads, bridges and airports suffered extensive damage during airstrikes and artillery attacks, and the shipping fleets of many countries were sunk.

The Marshall Plan for Recovery

The Marshall Plan was a four-year plan to reconstruct cities, industries and infrastructure that were heavily damaged during the war. This plan aimed also to remove trade barriers between European countries as well as foster commerce between those countries and the United States.

11The Marshall Plan, also known as the European Recovery Program, was a U.S. program providing aid to Western Europe following the devastation of World War II. It was enacted in 1948 and provided more than \$15 billion to help finance rebuilding efforts on the continent.11

The Marshall Plan was named after the U.S. Secretary of State George C. Marshall.

The Success of the Marshall Recovery Plan

12By and large, the Marshall Plan was successful in the recovery effort. It gave the needed boost to the European nations that were devastated by the six-year-long war.

As the designer of the plan, George C. Marshall himself said, “Our policy is not directed against any country but against hunger, poverty, desperation and chaos.”12

The countries that received funds under the plan didn’t have to repay the United States, as the monies were awarded in the form of grants. However, the countries did return roughly five per cent of the money to cover the administrative costs of the plan’s implementation. The Marshall Plan was a successful recovery plan that made Europe come back strongly after the war.
