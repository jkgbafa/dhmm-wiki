### Chapter 4\

DEFEAT STUBBORNNESS\
-----------------------------------------------

\

##### Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord.

##### 1 Corinthians 15:58

One of the battles you will have to win is the battle against stubbornness.

To defeat stubbornness, you must become more unyielding and resistant than your enemy.

1\. Stubborn and resistant enemies are only defeated by unrelenting resistance!

2\. Stubborn and resistant enemies are only defeated by adamant inflexibility!

3\. Defeat demonic stubbornness by being unmovable!

4\. Defeat stubbornness by outlasting the enemy!

5\. Defeat stubbornness by a relentless and long response!

Satan is a stubborn enemy! Indeed, from my experience, stubbornness is one of the foremost signs of demonic activity and presence. Satan has had thousands of years to apologise to the Heavenly Father for all the confusion he has caused. Our Heavenly Father is a loving and merciful God and I am sure there would have been some mercy and forgiveness. However, satan has not bothered to apologise or to back down from his relentless rebellion. Satan is an obstinate, adamant, hard-headed, wicked and stubborn personality. All forms of stubbornness come from the devil. Stubbornness is inspired by satan.

Remember that no one could bind the madman of Gadara. He was resistant to instructions, commands, advice and counsel. Usually, when you are dealing with a person who is taken up by evil spirits, you will notice that he is opinionated, headstrong and resistant to instructions, commands, advice, reason and even the use of force.

In marriages where you meet parties who are fixated on their stances, there is often a demonic element in the breakdown of the marriage.

One of the tasks of a Christian warrior is to defeat stubbornness by also being unmovable. Remember, the word of God teaches us to be unmovable! (1 Corinthians 15:58)

Your goal must be to destroy a stubborn enemy who is resistant to all advice and counsel.

Do not be shocked that you have to deal with Christians who will never change their minds no matter what. Such Christians are often demonized. Many Christians never change their minds about issues. Many married couples will never change their minds about the issues they are dealing with.

Enemies who refuse to settle disputes or be reasonable in their discussions are simply hosts of demons. Remember that the wisdom from above is easy to entreat, easy to advice, easy to counsel!

But the wisdom that is from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, and easy to be intreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality, and without hypocrisy.

James 3:17

Every war is started by a demonized and stubborn personality who will not yield to reason, threats, or counsel. Many lawsuits are initiated by capricious personalities who are both stubborn and unreasonable. Such personalities are doomed to destruction. Such people must be defeated by an equally strong and unyielding response.

He, that being often reproved hardeneth his neck, shall suddenly be destroyed, and that without remedy.

Proverbs 29:1

If you get more stubborn every time you are corrected, one day you will be crushed and never recover.

Proverbs 29:1 (GNB)

If you keep being stubborn after many warnings, you will suddenly discover you have gone too far.

Proverbs 29:1 (CEV)

This is a warning for all who are stubbornly fighting against the kingdom of God. You will be destroyed supernaturally!

We are dealing with a fiercely stubborn enemy. We must therefore approach any battle with the enemy as though it will last forever. You must never give up or slack in any way. It is only when you meet satan’s stubbornness with equal ferocity and determination that you can overcome him.

A Lesson from History

Stubborn people will only back down because they are absolutely defeated and not because they see reason or understand anything. Such was the experience with Adolf Hitler! He started the Second World War and never retreated from his goals of fighting against the Allies, until he killed himself. Through his unyielding stubbornness, Adolf Hitler caused the deaths of over 50 million people during the Second World War.

Though the Second World War ended with the death of Hitler, fighting continued between Japan and America, even after Hitler died. Japan entered the Second World War by bombing Pearl Harbour. The Japanese became an equally stubborn enemy who also caused the deaths of another 25 million people.

The Defeat of Stubborn Okinawa

America knew it could only defeat Japan by invading the Japanese mainland itself and defeating the Japanese on their own soil. To make this possible, America needed to control two strategic Japanese islands - Iwo Jima and Okinawa. These two islands were like stepping stones to the mainland of Japan and could be used as launching pads against the Japanese mainland itself if captured.

The invasion of Okinawa turned out to be the final battle of World War 2. The invasion of Okinawa could be compared to the invasion of Europe by Britain in what is called D-Day. However, the Okinawa invasion involved more than double the number of battle cruisers, destroyers and aircraft carriers that were used for the D-Day invasion of Europe in Normandy, France.

Indeed, the Okinawa invasion was bigger by far than the D-Day invasion and was incidentally called Love Day\*.

The island of Okinawa was the first real Japanese territory that the Americans fought on. It was 70 miles long and much bigger than the other strategic island, Iwo Jima. It had 250,000 Japanese citizens living on it, unlike the island Iwo Jima which had only military personnel on it.

Okinawa was defended by an Army of nearly 80,000 Japanese soldiers and 50,000 Navy ground troops and militia. The Americans sent over 180,000 troops onto the island. Okinawa was also defended by 1900 kamikaze suicide fighter planes that attacked the Americans and sank thirty-six ships in total.

The Americans were shocked by the suicidal behaviour of the Japanese in the war. No one expected the enemy to fly its planes straight into their ships. This suicidal kamikaze spirit emphasised to the Americans the reality that the Japanese were an unbending and extremely resistant enemy.

Stubbornness of the Okinawa Resistance

The Japanese fought fiercely to the very end. In the end, after 82 days of stubborn fighting, over 250,000 people were killed in Okinawa because of the stubborn resistance of the Japanese. Over a hundred thousand of these were Japanese soldiers.

The death toll from this terrible war on the island, as well as the brutality of the Okinawa soldiers discouraged the Americans from a land invasion of Japan. Yes, Okinawa was eventually defeated and captured but at a great cost to the Americans.

After the battle of Okinawa, instead of surrendering, Japan mustered up over one million troops to defend Kyushu, which was the part of mainland Japan that America would invade first. The Japanese were not discouraged by the Okinawa defeat. They fortified themselves to resist the expected invasion of the Japanese mainland. With over one million troops they intended to give the Americans a good blood bath, if they attempted to invade them. However, the Americans were not keen on a Kyushu land invasion after what happened in Okinawa. After the battle of Okinawa, all the American commanders believed that they would have as many as two million casualties if they tried to invade Japan.

The Americans had had enough of fighting with a stubborn and unreasonable enemy. Instead of invading Japan with conventional weapons, the Americans decided to deploy a nuclear bomb on Japan. They considered that it would save a lot of lives if they just dropped nuclear bombs on Japan.

Six weeks after the battle for Okinawa ended, the Americans used a nuclear weapon on Japan. That is the first and only time nuclear weapons have been used in the world.

Okinawa revealed the profound stubbornness and resistance of the Japanese army during the Second World War. The stubbornness of Japan in Okinawa revealed to the Americans that they were dealing with an intractable enemy who would never see reason nor surrender.

The End of Stubbornness

Fire-bombing was used against Japan to break their resistance. Firebombs were bombs that caused wildfires to break out and ferociously burn the city after the bomb exploded.

Towards the summer of 1945, Japan experienced the fire-bombing of Tokyo where over 100,000 people were killed and burnt from the American bombing raids. Tokyo, Osaka and Kobe were all fire-bombed by the Americans.

The Americans had also blocked ships from reaching Japan in order to starve the population. Japan was now starving because of the naval blockade which did not allow food or supplies to be brought to over 100 million of their citizens. Yet Japan would not surrender! They would prefer for everyone to die than for them to surrender.

Japan had leaders who were talking about “the suicide of an entire nation.” In other words, they were expecting the entire nation of 100 million people to commit suicide in defence of its land. In January 1945, in a radio broadcast, Lieutenant General Ryotaro Nakai called for the suicide of a hundred million people! Such statements reveal the kind of enemy and the kind of stubbornness the Americans encountered. Indeed, America had encountered a really stubborn enemy and needed extreme determination to overwhelm the suicidal, self-destructive tendencies of the Japanese military.

You will also encounter stubborn people in your ministry. There will be people who will prefer to destroy themselves than admit that they were ever wrong about anything. The end of stubborn people is their utter destruction!

It was hoped that the dropping of an atomic bomb on Hiroshima would make the Japanese give up, but it still did not make them surrender!

Perhaps, the Japanese thought that America had only one atomic bomb and still needed to be convinced that America had several such bombs and would deploy them if necessary.

After dropping the first atomic bomb on Hiroshima, America went ahead and dropped the second one on Nagasaki. It was only after the bombing of Nagasaki, the second city in Japan that Japan surrendered. Even then, the Japanese army commander still committed suicide.

Pearl Harbour was a devastating attack by Japan against the USA in 1941 but the response was an equally long-lasting war, including two atomic bombs against Japan. Clearly, the Japanese did not envisage the relentless and long response by the USA when they attacked Pearl Harbour. America was able to break the stubbornness of the Japanese army with an equally strong and overwhelming force.

\*“Love Day” (L-Day) on April 1, 1945, marked the start of the Allied invasion of Okinawa, also known as Operation Iceberg, and was chosen to avoid confusion with D-Day in Normandy.
