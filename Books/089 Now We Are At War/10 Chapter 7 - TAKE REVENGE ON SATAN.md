Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty; visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children’s children, unto the third and to the fourth generation.

Never pay back evil for evil to anyone. Respect what is right in the sight of all men. If possible, so far as it depends on you, be at peace with all men. Never take your own revenge, beloved, but leave room for the wrath of God, for it is written, “VENGEANCE IS MINE, I WILL REPAY,” says the Lord.

And the Lord said, Hear what the unjust judge saith. And shall not God avenge his own elect, which cry day and night unto him, though he bear long with them? I tell you that he will avenge them speedily. Nevertheless, when the Son of man cometh, shall he find faith on the earth?

Luke 18:2-8

Revenge by Obedience

Romans 5:19

The attacker then goes on endlessly invading, marauding, pillaging and plundering his victims. This is what Hitler did in Europe. Perhaps this is why God is a God of vengeance. Through vengeance, certain evils are stopped and blocked.

God is a God of vengeance! God believes in vengeance, that is why He calls Himself a God of vengeance. God believes in a strong retaliation!

Many of the European nations are nuclear powers with the capability of fighting terrible wars.

Hitler Unchecked

In March 1938, when Hitler invaded Austria there was no response to this invasion. In fact, Adolf Hitler was hailed as a saviour and a hero.

Then in September 1938, he invaded the Sudeten lands of Czechoslovakia. To this, Hitler received no proper reaction or response.

In March 1939, Hitler invaded the whole of Czechoslovakia and annexed it. To this invasion, Hitler still did not receive an appropriate reaction or response from the European nations. In other words, he was just allowed to take over a neighbouring nation.

On the 1st of September 1939, Adolf Hitler invaded Poland. When he invaded Poland, the whole world reacted; and that started the Second World War.

Unfortunately, Hitler was appeased every time he invaded and attacked a country. There should have been a stiff response to show him that he would not be allowed to invade and occupy neighbouring countries. As you can see, the lack of retaliation and revenge caused Hitler to grow bolder and seize more territory. Retaliation and revenge cause wicked and malevolent enemies to stop bullying the weaker ones.

The Revenge of America for Pearl Harbour

It is America’s response to Japan’s attack that stopped the Japanese from invading the whole world. Revenge is important in stopping enemies!

Japan attacked America in Pearl Harbour. This was a devastating, never-to-be-forgotten attack. Eighteen ships were damaged and over 2,400 people were killed. America retaliated in many ways against Japan.

1\. The Revenge for Pearl Harbour - Declaring war.

America avenged itself by declaring war against Japan which had harmed them without provocation.

It is important to declare war on those who have attacked you without provocation. That is how wars begin and you must be ready to begin a war if necessary. America retaliated against the bombing of Pearl Harbour by declaring war on Japan. After declaring war, there were several retaliatory attacks on Japan.

2\. The Revenge for Pearl Harbour - The Doolittle raid.

America retaliated by bombing Japan in the Tokyo strikes. The first revenge attack by America on Japan was called the Doolittle Raid.

“The Doolittle Raid”, also known as the Tokyo Raid, was an air raid on 18 April 1942 by the United States on the Japanese capital Tokyo during World War II. It was the first American air operation to strike Japan. Although the raid caused comparatively minor damage, it demonstrated that the Japanese mainland was vulnerable to American air attacks. The raid was planned and led by Lieutenant Colonel James Doolittle of the US Army and that is why it was named the Doolittle Raid.

Perhaps, the Japanese felt safe because Japan was so far from America; but the Doolittle attack served as a warning to the Japanese public that America was capable of striking back in Japan. After the Japanese attacked Pearl Harbour on 7 December 1941, America needed to prove to Japan that they were not out of range from any retaliation.

During the Doolittle operation, 16 aircrafts were launched from the carrier USS Hornet and bombed targets in Japan. America lost three of the aircrew but 13 of them reached safety in China afterwards.

3\. The Revenge for Pearl Harbour - Midway Island.

America avenged itself of Pearl Harbour at the Battle of Midway. The Battle of Midway was a major naval battle that took place from 4th to 7th of June 1942, six months after Japan’s attack on Pearl Harbour.

Midway Island is halfway between America and Asia and was an important American territory used as a convenient refuelling spot for trans-Pacific flights and navy ships.

The Japanese, during the Second World War, intended to occupy Midway Island and further destroy the American navy and aircraft carriers. Japanese military planners hoped to defeat the US Pacific Fleet and take Midway, from which they could launch additional attacks on Pearl Harbor, secure dominance in the region and then force a negotiated peace.

The Japanese plan was faulty because they assumed that the Americans would not be able to respond properly and respond as they should. Most significantly, American cryptographers were able to determine the date and location of the planned attack, enabling the forewarned U.S. Navy to prepare its own ambush.

The sea and air battle for Midway Island lasted four days and the US Navy successfully destroyed four Japanese aircraft carriers and defeated the imperial Japanese navy while losing only one of its own aircraft carriers, The Yorktown, to the previously invincible Japanese navy.

After this battle, Japan did not have the capacity to replace their aircraft carriers or train men rapidly enough. This defeat and destruction of the Japanese navy at Midway was considered a major turning point in the world war.

4\. The Revenge for Pearl Harbour - Fire-bombing Tokyo.

America revenged the attack on Pearl Harbour by fire-bombing Tokyo. The Bombing of Tokyo was a series of fire-bombing air raids by the United States Army Air Force during World War II.

Fire-bombing is a bombing technique that is intended to damage the target more by the fire than by the explosian. Operation Meetinghouse, conducted on the night of 9 – 10 March 1945, is the single most destructive bombing raid in human history. 16 square miles (10,000 acres) of central Tokyo was destroyed, leaving an estimated 100,000 civilians dead and over one million homeless.

Even the nuclear atomic bombing of Nagasaki caused fewer immediate deaths than the fire-bombing of Tokyo. The fire-bombing of the downtown Tokyo suburb of Shitamachi was very effective. There were 750,000 people living in cramped quarters in wooden buildings. 334 bombers flying at a height of just 500 feet dropped their incendiary bombs, creating a giant bonfire in Shitamachi, which spread throughout Tokyo.

5\. The Revenge for Pearl Harbour - The nuclear bombs.

America avenged itself of Pearl Harbour by attacking Hiroshima and Nagasaki with nuclear bombs. Hiroshima was a major port and also a military headquarters. Nagasaki was a major port for trade and a centre for information on western technology and science.

US President, Harry Truman, ordered the atomic bomb to be dropped on Nagasaki to force Japan to surrender. President Truman justified his bombing of Hiroshima and Nagasaki by pointing to the unprovoked Japanese attack on Pearl Harbour and the murder of American prisoners.

As you can see, revenge on your attackers is essential if you are going to avoid repeated walkover invasions by terrorists, bullies and dictators. You must become someone who can also attack back after you have been attacked, otherwise you will be the subject of incessant, unprovoked and wicked attacks.
