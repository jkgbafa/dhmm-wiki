### Chapter 3\

A Warning from History and Tradition\
----------------------------------------------------------

For I know this, that AFTER MY DEPARTING SHALL GRIEVOUS WOLVES ENTER IN among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them. Therefore watch, and remember, that by the space of three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears.

Acts 20:29-31

Each of the fictional stories in this chapter will teach you a valuable lesson about wolves. Wolves are important to us because of what Jesus Christ and Apostle Paul taught about the danger of wolves to the church. The wolf is the ultimate villain in many traditional and historical tales.

In folklore and in the Bible, the wolf is always a symbol of evil, savagery and treachery. Many of the stories about wolves tell us of the ill will, the deception, the treachery, the savagery, the wickedness, the cruelty, the manipulation and the trickery of wolves. It is these characteristics that Jesus sought to remind us of when He spoke about shepherds protecting the sheep from wolves.

It is these very wolf characteristics that Paul sought to make us aware of when he predicted that wolves would come into the church. Apostle Paul wanted us to know that such wolf-like characters were going to arise in the church and would manifest the legendary deception, treachery, savagery, wickedness, cruelty, manipulation and trickery of wolves!

1. THE WOLF AND THE THREE LITTLE PIGS: EXPECT WOLVES, PREPARE FOR WOLVES.

Be sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour:

1 Peter 5:8

The story of the wolf and the three little pigs teaches the importance of hard work and preparation in relation to wolf attacks. The wolf wanted to eat up the three little pigs. The pigs were juicy lunch for the wolf. Wolves will eat the sheep and the flocks. Wolves will not care what happens to your life’s work.

Wolves are real! The wolves will come! Spiritual and ministerial wolves are real! The wolves will attempt to kill, to steal and to destroy your flock.

Every church must prepare for the coming of the wolves. Wolves are wicked pastors who devour the flock of God and scatter the sheep. Churches that do not teach loyalty and faithfulness will struggle to survive a wolf attack. Churches that just teach only on power and miracles will not easily overcome a wolf attack.

The Three Little Pigs and the Wolf

Once upon a time, there were three little pigs, who left their mummy and daddy to see the world. All summer long, they roamed through the woods and over the plains, playing games and having fun. None were happier than the three little pigs, and they easily made friends with everyone. Wherever they went, they were given a warm welcome, but as summer drew to a close, they realized that folks were drifting back to their usual jobs and preparing for winter.

Autumn came and it began to rain. The three little pigs started to feel they needed a real home. Sadly, they knew that the fun was over, and they must set to work like the others, or they would be left in the cold and rain, with no roof over their heads. They talked about what to do, but in the end, each decided for himself.

The first little pig said he would build a straw hut.

“It will only take a day,” he said. The others disagreed.

“It’s too fragile,” they said disapprovingly, but he refused to listen.

The second little pig built a house of wood. He went in search of planks of seasoned wood. It took him two days to nail them together.

“That’s not the way to build a house!” the third little pig said. “It takes time, patience and hard work to build a house that is strong enough to withstand the wind, the rain, and the wolves!”

The third little pig decided to build a house out of bricks. The days went by, and the wisest little pig’s brick house took shape. Brick by brick and block by block!

From time to time, the two other little pigs visited him and mocked him saying, “Why are you working so hard? Why don’t you come and play?”

But the third little pig said, “I shall finish my house first. It must be solid and sturdy. And then I’ll come and play!”

One day, the little pigs found the tracks of a big wolf in the neighbourhood. The three little pigs rushed home in alarm because they heard that the big wolf had decided to hunt and eat some pigs for lunch.

When the wolf saw the first little pig’s straw hut, he ordered the pig to come out to be eaten. But the frightened little pig refused to come out.

“If you don’t come out, I will huff and puff and blow your house down.” So, he huffed and puffed and blew the first little pig’s house down. When the house fell down the first little pig rushed out to hide in his brother’s house which was made of wood.

So, the wolf ran after him to the second house where the two little pigs were hiding. Inside, the two brothers wept in fear and did their best to hold the door fast against the wolf’s blows.

“If you don’t open this door, I will huff and puff and blow your house down.” So, the wolf huffed and puffed and blew the second little pig’s wooden house down. The two little pigs rushed out and ran to the third little pig’s house which was made of bricks.

The wolf ran after the little pigs who had just managed to enter the third little pig’s house. This house of the third little pig was much more solid than the other two. The wolf stood outside and commanded them to open the door, but they refused. So, the wolf blew and blew and huffed and puffed but the third little pig’s house stood firm because it was made of bricks.

The wolf was very hungry and decided to try something else. He scrambled up a nearby ladder, to enter the house through the chimney. However, the wisest little pig decided to light a fire below the chimney. As the wolf slid into the house through the chimney, he fell straight into the fire the little pig had prepared for him. The flames licked his hairy coat, and his tail became a flaring torch. The wolf ran away as fast as it could, aflame with fire.

The three happy little pigs, dancing round and round the yard, began to sing and dance. From that terrible day on, the wisest little pig’s brothers set to work to build two new brick houses.

The wolf did return once to roam in the neighbourhood, but when he caught sight of the three brick houses with three chimneys, he remembered the terrible pain and fire he had experienced there and never went back.

This story teaches us that we must expect a wolf to attack one day. We must prepare for the wolves. Victory loves preparation! Our victory over every wolf that will arise depends on how we prepare the flock to defend itself from the coming of the wolf.

2\. LITTLE RED RIDING HOOD: WOLVES WILL LEAD YOU ASTRAY.

Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.

Ephesians 6:11

Little Red Riding Hood

Once upon a time, there was a sweet little girl who always wore a red hooded cloak, so everyone called her Little Red Riding Hood. One day, her mother gave her a basket of food and said, “Take this to your grandmother, but stay on the path and don’t talk to strangers!”

As she walked through the forest, she met a sly wolf. The wolf asked where she was going, and innocent Little Red Riding Hood told him about her grandmother’s cottage. The wolf, being cunning, suggested she pick some flowers for her grandmother while he took a shortcut to the cottage.

The wolf arrived first, tricked the grandmother into opening her door, and swallowed her whole. Then he put on her nightgown and cap, climbed into bed, and waited for Little Red Riding Hood.

When she arrived at her grandmother’s cottage Little Red Riding Hood noticed something was off. She said, “Grandmother, what big ears you have!”

The big bad wolf said, “The better to hear you with, my dear.”

Little Red Riding Hood said, “Grandmother, what big eyes you have!”

The big bad wolf said, “The better to see you with, my dear.”

Little Red Riding Hood said, “Grandmother, what big teeth you have!”

The big bad wolf said, “The better to eat you with!”

With that, the wolf leapt from the bed and swallowed Little Red Riding Hood whole.

Fortunately, a passing woodsman heard the commotion and rushed in. Seeing the wolf’s full belly, he cut it open, freeing Little Red Riding Hood and her grandmother. They then filled the wolf’s stomach with heavy stones, and when he awoke and tried to run away, he collapsed and died. From then on, Little Red Riding Hood promised never to stray from the path or talk to strangers again. And they all lived happily ever after!

The story of Little Red Riding Hood is another famous story which brings to the fore the trickery and danger that wolves pose. This innocent girl was fooled by the wolf to go astray from her mission. She was on a mission to deliver food to her grandmother, but she strayed off course and began to play in the forest.

O, how much danger we are in when we begin to go astray and not focus on our God-given mission! We also see how the wolf dressed up and pretended to be her grandmother.

Wolves will come with trickery and manipulation. They will seek to trick you into allowing them to come deeper and closer so that they can strike at you.

3\. THE WOLF AND THE MOUNTAIN GOAT: WOLVES WILL ATTEMPT TO FOOL YOU.

Lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices.

2 Corinthians 2:11

The story of the wolf and the goat teaches the lesson of ignoring the deception and manipulation of a wolf. A wolf will seek to make you do things as though it is for your benefit. Perhaps, a wolf minister may receive an offering when it is actually for his benefit and not for yours. Perhaps a wolf will take you in to counsel you but end up having sex with you and abuse you as part of the counselling. He is a wolf and not a shepherd! Shepherds do not devour their sheep!

The Story of the Wolf and the Mountain Goat

Once upon a time, a wolf saw a goat grazing at the edge of a high cliff. The wolf smacked his lips at the thought of a fine goat dinner.

“My dear friend,” said the wolf in his sweetest voice, “Aren’t you afraid you will fall from that cliff? Come down here and graze on this fine grass beside me on safe, level ground.”

“No, thank you,” said the goat.

“Well then,” said the wolf, “Aren’t you cold up there in the wind? You would be warmer grazing down here beside me in this sheltered area.”

“No, thank you,” said the goat.

“But the grass tastes better down here!” said the exasperated wolf, “Why dine alone?”

“My dear wolf,” the goat finally said, “Are you quite sure that it is my dinner you are worrying about and not your own? You want to eat me! You don’t love me! You want to have me! You want to destroy me! You have never cared for me! I will never come down from this high mountain.”

Once again, we learn not to be taken in by the tricks of a wolf. Wolf pastors present themselves as the most caring and most loving pastors. In reality they do not care for you! In reality, these “loving” pastors are wicked, ravenous and treacherous men.

4\. THE WOLF IN SHEEP’S CLOTHING: WOLVES ARE ACTORS AND PRETENDERS.

Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep’s clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves. Ye shall know them by their fruits. Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles?

Matthew 7:15-16

The Story of the Wolf in Sheep’s Clothing

Once upon a time, a wolf found a sheep’s skin. He wrapped himself in it and sneaked into a large sheep’s pen. That night he ate one of the lambs in the sheep’s pen. He stayed a whole week in the sheep’s pen and ate a lamb every night.

The next week, he was sneaking up to eat another lamb when the shepherd caught him.

“Oh, please don’t throw me out,” said the wolf. “I’m one of your sheep. All these sheep are my brethren.”

But the shepherd was not fooled by the wolf in sheep’s clothing.

“You’re only pretending to be a sheep,” said the shepherd. “You cannot fool me! I know you’re a wolf.”

“How do you know?” asked the wolf. “I look like a sheep.”

“You say you are a sheep,” said the shepherd, “but you act like a wolf.” With that the shepherd pulled a branch from a tree and beat up the wolf with it. The wolf leapt over the fence and climbed a nearby hill. He never went back to that sheep pen because he was terrified of the good shepherd! He never wanted to receive such a terrible beating again.

A good shepherd recognizes wolves and fights them. Wolves do not like good shepherds because it is good and experienced shepherds who warn their sheep about wolves. It is good and experienced shepherds who fiercely drive away wolves.

5\. THE WOLF AND THE HOUSE DOG: WOLVES CAN NOT BE TRAINED.

Submitting yourselves one to another in the fear of God.

Ephesians 5:21

In this story, we see a comparison between a house dog and a wolf. The house dog can be trained, chained and controlled peacefully. A wolf will not and cannot be restricted, chained, controlled or tamed. A wild wolf does not want the benefits of being part of a family. It is a wild animal which cannot be trained or re-trained.

All your attempts to integrate certain people into the church family will fail because you are dealing with wolves and not house dogs. Wolves are wild animals and will never appreciate the peace and security of the church family.

The Story of the Wolf and the House Dog

Once upon a time, there lived a powerful wolf and a large house dog. The wolf and the house dog were cousins but lived in separate places. Occasionally, they would meet and discuss their lives.

One day, the wolf was discouraged because he couldn’t catch any animals to eat in the forest. Discouraged after that unsuccessful day of hunting, the hungry wolf came upon the well-fed, large, powerful house dog who was passing by.

“Ah, Cousin,” said the wolf. “How do you get food to eat regularly? You look so relaxed and well-fed.”

The wolf asked the house dog, “What do you do to get all this food you are enjoying?”

“Very little,” replied the house dog, “I just protect my master’s house and family, and I am obedient to his demands.”

The wolf pondered this quite carefully – for he had to risk his own life, hunting almost daily, to earn his food. Many times, the wolf’s hunt for food ended without success. The wolf was tempted to adopt the dog’s mode of living.

The house dog asked the wolf, “Why do you not humble yourself and work for someone as I do? That way you will be given your food regularly.”

“I would have no objection,” said the wolf, “If I could only get a place.”

“I will easily arrange that for you,” said the house dog. “Come with me to my master and you shall share my work.”

So, the wolf and the dog went towards the town together. On the way there, the wolf noticed that the hair on a certain part of the house dog’s neck was very much worn away, so he asked him how that had come about.

“Oh, it is nothing,” said the dog. “That is only the place where the collar is put on my neck to keep me chained up. It’s a bit uncomfortable but one soon gets used to it.”

The wolf abruptly stopped and exclaimed, “Your chain! You mean you are chained in the house? What? Does that mean that you are not free to come and go as you please?”

“No,” responded the dog, “But what does that matter? I am trained very well every day! I have a home, and I am well fed every day! I feel blessed as a house dog.”

Then the wolf said, “It matters to me. I don’t want any training! I can’t stand being restricted in any way. I can’t even imagine a chain around my neck. I do not care if I go hungry for weeks on end. I prefer to suffer than to be part of that family.”

“Goodbye,” said the wolf to the house dog. And the wolf never came back asking to become a house dog.

Indeed, there are people who will never be happy to be part of a family. They are simply meant to be wild and untrainable. They do not want any restrictions or rules. They do not want to be the topic of any meetings, discussions, restrictions or corrections. They do not want to answer any questions about their treachery or any evil they have done. Such people should be left alone to be wild wolves and to roam the forest alone. Do not try to make them part of the family!

6\. THE WOLF AND THE BONE: WOLVES ARE UNGRATEFUL.

For people will love only themselves and their money. They will be boastful and proud, scoffing at God, disobedient to their parents, and UNGRATEFUL. They will consider nothing sacred.

2 Timothy 3:2 (NLT)

Once upon a time, there was a wolf living in the forest. This wolf went hunting and caught an animal for lunch. Whilst the wolf was munching on the animal he had killed, a small bone in the meat suddenly got stuck in his throat and he could not swallow it.

He soon felt a terrible pain in his throat, and ran up and down groaning and moaning, whilst seeking something to relieve the pain. The distressed wolf tried to persuade everyone he met to help him remove the bone.

“I would give anything,” said he, “If you would take it out.”

But most of the animals would not have anything to do with the wolf because he was not liked. The wicked wolf was well known for killing other animals. No one trusted him and no one wanted to help him!

The wolf then offered a big reward to anyone who would help him remove the bone from his throat. At last, the crane who had a long neck and a sharp pointed beak, was persuaded to try and help the wolf remove the bone. After all, he would get a big reward for this.

The crane then told the wolf to lie on his side and open his jaws as wide as he could. The crane put his long neck down the wolf’s throat, and with his beak loosened the bone, till at last, he got it out.

The wolf was very relieved.

The crane then asked for his reward.

“Will you kindly give me the reward you promised?” asked the crane.

The wolf grinned, showing his brilliant white razor teeth. He snapped, “Be content. You have put your head inside a wolf’s mouth and taken it out again safely! You should be thankful that I did not bite off your head. Please consider that as your reward.”

With that, the ungrateful wolf sauntered off, forgetting so soon how no one had been willing to help him till the crane came along and risked his life to help him.

Always remember that wolves are ungrateful and wicked men. They are unthankful for all that has been done for them. They will not remember the kindness that has been shown to them in the past.

7\. THE WOLF AND THE FEAST OF LAMBS: WOLVES DECEIVE THE SIMPLE-MINDED.

The simple believeth every word: but the prudent man looketh well to his going.

Proverbs 14:15

Once upon a time, there lived a pack of wolves that hunted often in a nearby forest where a large flock of sheep and lambs grazed peacefully in a pasture. The sheep, until then, had grazed in safety, protected by dogs. The dogs barked and chased away the wolves anytime the wolves came near the flock.

One day, the wolves shouted to the sheep from the forest where they were hiding.

The wolves said to the flock of sheep, “Oh sheep we live in the same area but we have never been close!”

The wolves added, “We would get along so well if it were not for those hostile and unfriendly dogs with you. We are supposed to be a family and to live peacefully together in this area. Send the dogs away and you’ll see that we will become such good friends. Our children will play together and become lifelong friends.”

The sheep were easily fooled and agreed. They sent all the dogs away. That very night, the wolves entered the pasture and devoured many of the sheep in a great feast.

8\. THE WOLF, THE LION AND THE FOX: WOLVES ARE FULL OF ILL WILL.

Therefore, get rid of all ill will and all deceit, pretense, envy, and slander.

1 Peter 2:1 (CEB)

Once upon a time, there lived an old sick lion who lay resting in a big cave. All the beasts in the forest came to visit their king to pay their respects to the lion. The fox was the only animal who did not find the time to visit the lion king. The wolf, therefore, thinking that he had a good opportunity, accused the fox to the lion.

The wolf said, “Oh, great king, please take note that the fox has not bothered to pay his respects to you. He is the only animal who has not come to visit you since you got sick.”

“Oh,” said the lion, “I had not even noticed that the fox had not been to visit me since I fell sick.”

That very day, the fox came to visit the lion. Before he went in to see the lion, he was told what the wolf had said about him.

The lion roared out in anger when he saw the “ungrateful” and “disrespectful” fox who had not bothered to visit him since he fell sick. But the fox defended himself and said, “Oh, great king, the reason I have not visited you is not that I didn’t love you but rather I was seeking a cure for you. I have gone around the whole world seeking medicines and cures just to help you, my lord. And my lord, King Lion, I am pleased to let you know that I have indeed found a cure for you.”

“Really? A cure for me?” The old lion was immediately interested and commanded the fox to tell him the cure.

The fox answered, “You must flay a wolf alive and wrap his warm skin around you.”

“Catch the wolf,” the lion said, “And flay him alive. It will be a cure for me,” said the lion king.

The wolf was at once taken and flayed. While they were taking the wolf to flay him, the fox turned to him, and said with a smile, “You should not have accused me to the king. You were full of ill will and malice towards me. Now it has cost you your life!”

May a slanderer not endure on the earth; May evil hunt a violent person violently.

Psalm 140:11 (NASB)

Wolves are accusers! They point fingers and accuse you of wrongdoing. They will tell the whole world their evil stories and wicked defamatory lies. Note that the lies that this wolf spoke about the fox could have cost the fox his life. The accusation backfired on the wolf, because accusations and lies will ultimately backfire.

The trouble they make for others backfires on them. The violence they plan falls on their own heads.

Psalm 7:16 (NLT)
