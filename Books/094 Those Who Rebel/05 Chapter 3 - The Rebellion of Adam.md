### Chapter 3\

The Rebellion of Adam\
-------------------------------------------

And when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was pleasant to the eyes, and a tree to be desired to make one wise, she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat. And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked; and they sewed fig leaves together, and made themselves aprons. And they heard the voice of the LORD God walking in the garden in the cool of the day: and Adam and his wife hid themselves from the presence of the LORD God amongst the trees of the garden.

Genesis 3:6-8

Adam was a rebel in the sense of having broken a command, but not in the bold, defiant way rebels often behave. Adam was not a revolutionary or someone seeking to overthrow God, like Lucifer. Adam did not challenge God’s authority. Adam fell after a moment of weakness or curiosity, influenced by the temptation from Eve and the serpent.

However, although Adam’s actions seem more passive, Adam was also rebellious because he disobeyed God’s clear instructions. That is why Adam went into hiding after sinning against God’s command. Adam’s rebellion began mankind’s transformation into the wretched state we are now in.

Adam was the first human created by God and placed in the Garden of Eden with one clear rule: do not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. Eve, persuaded by the serpent, ate the fruit and shared it with Adam. Adam also ate it, consciously defying God’s command.

A rebel is someone who defies authority or established rules. Adam had a direct instruction from God and chose to go against it, making it an act of disobedience and rebellion. Adam’s sin has three important elements which make it rebellion.

1\. Disobedience: God explicitly commanded Adam not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, but he ate the fruit anyway.

And the Lord God commanded the man, saying, Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.

Genesis 2:16-17

2\. Defiance: By knowingly rejecting God’s command, Adam rebelled against divine authority.

3\. Open disregard: Adam deviated from the course given to him and quietly moved away from God into a life of rebellion and defiance against God’s word.

Adam’s act is the original sin of mankind and the sin with the greatest effects on humanity. Today, that tendency to reject and defy God’s will has been passed on to the whole human race.
