### CHAPTER 5\

Ready@20 Just Like the Levites\
====================================================

And the Lord spake unto Moses, saying, This is it that belongeth unto the Levites: FROM TWENTY AND FIVE YEARS OLD AND UPWARD THEY SHALL GO IN TO WAIT UPON THE SERVICE OF THE TABERNACLE OF THE CONGREGATION: AND FROM THE AGE OF FIFTY YEARS THEY SHALL CEASE WAITING UPON THE SERVICE THEREOF, and shall serve no more: But shall minister with their brethren in the tabernacle of the congregation, to keep the charge, and shall do no service. Thus shalt thou do unto the Levites touching their charge.

Numbers 8:23-26

You must be ready@20 to be a Levite! Levites were supposed to start work from the age of twenty-five and keep working until they were fifty. Another scripture teaches that people should work in the ministry from the age of thirty and upwards. “From thirty years old and upward even unto fifty years old, every one that entereth into the service, for the work in the tabernacle of the congregation,” (Numbers 4:39)

The Law and the Prophets are teaching us what is important. The Law and the Prophets are teaching you the ages at which you will be the most useful to God. This is an important revelation. I had a spiritual father called Uncle James. He shared this amazing revelation that the Levites were supposed to work from twenty-five to fifty.

It struck a chord in my heart and I knew that I must serve the Lord as a young man. It is important to assume that you will have to retire from God’s work at the age of fifty. When you know that you will retire at the age of fifty, you work with all your heart and might. You give yourself to the task God has laid before you.

The truth is that the most fruitful years of your life are between twenty-five and fifty. Every woman is most fertile between the ages of twenty-five and fifty. Usually, after the age of fifty she is not able to have children. This is because she has ceased to be fertile, fruitful and productive after the age of fifty.

Nature is teaching us what the most fertile and fruitful period of your life is. “Doth not even nature itself teach you, ...?” (1 Corinthians 11:14).

Nature is an amazing teacher. Nature teaches you what to do, what to expect and when to expect it. Nature teaches you that you will be most productive for God between twenty-five and fifty. God wants to use you when you are in your twenties, thirties and forties. Unfortunately, many people want to give themselves to God when they are in their forties and fifties and in the twilight of their lives.

Do not believe it when they say, “Life begins at forty.” Indeed, life begins at twenty! You must be ready@20! You must work for God in your twenties. Do not wait till you are forty! There are many things you can do at twenty. At twenty you are better at fighting and dodging evil.

Although you have many more desires and longings as a young man, your ability to fight back is greater. Although there are many strong desires in your youth, you are more able to dodge evil. You can pray longer when you are a young man. It takes strength to pray. You can fast longer when you are a young man. It takes strength to fast!

I know a grown-up who died during a three-day fast. The three-day fast was too much for her. She developed an illness during the fast and died. If she were younger, she probably would have had a better chance to survive. It is important to know that there are many things a young body can do better. Between twenty-five and fifty you can be better at marriage! Between twenty-five and fifty you can do the ministry better! Between twenty-five and fifty you can build a church better.

I remember a brother who set out to build a church. After a while, his church was not doing well and it was not growing. He decided to branch off into motivational speaking and writing books for students. Even though he was a fine minister, his church planting mission was failing because he had started his ministry too late. He was now a middle-aged man, a little slower, a little less sharp, a little more hesitant and a little less in touch with new technology. It is not easy to see how huge changes come upon a person as they grow older.

I once had the privilege of being on a flight with a football star who had played in the English Premier League. He was so famous that his name had become a household name and all the fans had a pet name for him. He was a little older when I spoke to him. I asked him whether his ability to play had changed as he had grown older.

He nodded, “Yes.”

“Why?” I asked, “What exactly has changed?”

He said, “Now, when I shoot at the goal, the shots are not as accurate as they used to be. The ball would go a little to the right instead of going straight. When I was younger, my shots were sharper and straighter.” He was describing many little changes that had taken place as he grew a little older.

The changes in your body are not easy to detect.

A seventy-year old minister told me that he had handed over his church to a younger person when he was sixty years old. He said, “There is a difference between when I was sixty years old and when I was twenty-five.” He said, “When I was twenty-five years old I had the zeal and the desire to build the church. I would search for any material I could lay my hands on that would help me to build the church.

As I got older, the quality of my decisions changed. I made more mistakes. I had less passion for the ministry. I felt more and more that everything was hopeless. I became more conscious of the fact that people do not change when you preach to them.”

You will notice that Solomon had three different books ascribed to him. The Songs of Solomon shows him at the youngest stage of his ministry. He was full of love. He was full of passion. He was full of zeal.

The book of Proverbs shows him full of wisdom and practical common sense. The book of Proverbs shows him more in the middle stages of his life, probably from forty to fifty years.

As Solomon grew older, he became more philosophical. He reflected more on the realities of life and that is when he wrote the book of Ecclesiastes. In this book, he almost sounds hopeless about the situations in the world. He reflects on how people work so hard and all they get in return is envy. Solomon notes how vain and futile life on earth is. He calls life on earth “life under the sun”. You must have heard the phrase “vanity of vanities, all is vanity.”

Indeed, after the age of fifty, you will be saying, “All is vanity.” You will notice the futilities more than you notice the possibilities. You will see the tinge of sadness and hopelessness in people’s eyes. You will see the hopelessness all around. You will long for the coming of Jesus. You will hope for the end of the world. You will sense impending catastrophe all the time.

It is not easy to build great things for God when your mind is working in the Ecclesiastes mode. You cannot work for God and say it is vanity of vanities. You need to be in the Songs of Solomon mode if you want to build great things for God. You need to be full of love and passion to serve God. You must really love God with youthfulness and strength to serve Him beautifully.

To be a good wife, you need to be youthful and strong so that you can serve your husband with comforts, softness and delights that will make him miss you when you are not with him. When you are tired and retired, you will not be able to serve him with comforts, softness and delights.

You need to have a fresh anointing! You need to have zeal! You need a drive! You need to be sacrificial! You need to be relentless! You need to pursue and overtake what lies ahead! You need to be hopeful! All these beautiful qualities will surface naturally when you are between twenty-five and fifty.
