### CHAPTER 7\

Ready@20 to Serve\
=======================================

AND MOSES AND AARON WERE BROUGHT AGAIN UNTO PHARAOH: AND HE SAID UNTO THEM, GO, SERVE THE LORD YOUR GOD: BUT WHO ARE THEY THAT SHALL GO?

AND MOSES SAID, WE WILL GO WITH OUR YOUNG and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds will we go; for we must hold a feast unto the Lord. And he said unto them, Let the Lord be so with you, as I will let you go, and your little ones: look to it; for evil is before you. Not so: go now ye that are men, and serve the Lord; for that ye did desire. And they were driven out from Pharaoh’s presence.

Exodus 10:8-11

You must be ready@20 to serve! Moses refused to go out of Egypt without the young people. Moses declared that all the young people would be going along with him on the mission. Why were young people so necessary for Moses? Young people have important qualities that are helpful in the ministry. Instead of seeing young people as irrelevant, it is important to see them as an indispensable part of ministry.

1\. Young people are needed for their music, dance, flow and joy!

And Moses said, We will go with our young and with our old, with our sons and with our daughters, with our flocks and with our herds will we go; for we must hold a feast unto the LORD.

Exodus 10:9

The music will change, the dance will change, the flow will change and the joy level will change when there are young people. Young people are indispensable.

Every church that does not use its young people is making a mistake. I once attended a church that had some very funky, young-sounding music. The choir was located a little behind where I was sitting, so I did not turn my head to see who was singing.

Somehow, I assumed there was a young trendy group singing for us that evening. When I turned my head, I was shocked to find about twenty grandmothers or great-grandmothers jamming away. I had never seen such elderly grey-haired women singing and playing that kind of music. Almost every one of them had grey or white hair. It was a sight to behold.

I wondered where the young people were. Indeed, the church was half empty that night. Apparently young people did not do much in that church. Later on, I found out that most of the young people were given to playing basketball instead of being involved in church activities.

A church cannot grow and move forward without young people. Growth always comes about by the arrival of children. Without children you can never say your church is growing. This is why Moses insisted on going with the young as well as the old. Moses knew that he needed the wisdom of the old but he also needed the contribution of the young.

2\. Young people are needed for their strength.

The glory of young men is their strength: and the beauty of old men is the gray head.

Proverbs 20:29

The glory of young people is their strength. This means that the beauty or the nice part of young people is their strength. The strength of a young person is seen in his determination to serve God. Many young people are full of zeal and unwavering determination to follow Jesus.

It is young people who believe that they can go out into the world and into the uttermost parts of the earth. Older people consider the Great Commission to go into the whole world as something far-fetched. Older people consider many of the instructions of Jesus as fanciful and unrealistic statements that cannot be followed by a normal person.

What are some of the instructions and statements of Jesus?

Many older people think that a person must be mad to follow such “weird instructions”.

Indeed, it is young people who will receive these instructions and try hard to obey them. The older ones will just smile sheepishly and say, “I am sure your father has a lot of money; that is why you are thinking of being in full time missions.”

Without young people, many parts of the Bible will not be obeyed. Without young people, many sections of the Bible may not even be considered as true.

3\. Young people are needed to bear the yoke.

It is good for a man that he bear the yoke in his youth.

Lamentations 3:27

There are many yokes that you have to bear in order to be successful in the ministry. Young people must serve the Lord now so that it is easier for them when they are older. Could it be that an old person who is working extra hours with hard labour did not bear his yoke in his youth?

It is better for a man to bear the yoke when he is young. What is the “yoke”? The “yoke” is the hard and difficult part of your life’s assignment. It is important that you take up the hard and difficult aspect of your life’s assignment as a child.

5 Mark 16:17

6 Mark 16:18

7 Luke 9:3

8 Matthew 6:19-20

If God has asked you to start a church, the hard and difficult part is the laying of the foundation of the church. Starting a church is more difficult than pastoring an existing church. If you have been called to be an evangelist, organising the actual campaign is more difficult than preaching.

I once met a seventy-year old evangelist. I asked him whether he would be able to continue preaching at crusades.

He answered emphatically, “Yes! I can preach at crusades.”

He went on and explained, “I cannot organise the campaigns as I used to. I cannot do the administration, the fundraising, the advance work, the mobilisation, the publicity, the pastoral interactions, the governmental interactions and all the protocols that have to be observed for a crusade to come on. But I can still preach,” he emphasized. Indeed, this evangelist was clearly outlining “the yoke” that an evangelist bears. The yoke of an evangelist is heavy and it is good for an evangelist to bear this yoke when he is young.

One day, I met a brother who was about to start a church. He was past his middle age and had put on a lot of weight. Due to different circumstances, he had been forced to plant a church at this stage of his life. I knew him from the time he was a young man. He was an anointed pastor and someone with a genuine call of God. However, I was a little worried about him pioneering a church at this stage of his life.

I was concerned because he would have to find a hall in the right location and rent it. He would need to find chairs, instruments, a drum set, an organ and a projector. This middle-aged man would have to go out on the streets and do door-to-door evangelism, person-to-person evangelism, and house-to-house invitations to bring people to church.

He would also have to organise breakfast meetings, open-air crusades, stand at street corners and hand out tracts to passers-by. He would have to find a designer to design a logo, a leaflet, handbills and contact the nearest printer to print leaflets advertising his church. He would have to make a pulpit and set up a banner for his new church.

This new pastor would have to conduct services with very few people at the beginning of his ministry. He would have to go through several Sundays during which very few people would practically attend his services. Indeed, he would have to find the finances to support his new church. He would probably need supporters to give him money. All these experiences are difficult yokes for a middle-aged man to bear.

A young person will, however, go through these experiences with ease. It is far better for a young man to bear the yoke in his youth! In the ministry, there are yokes that are heavy burdens of ministry. There are also lighter and easier sections of ministry. When people do not accept to bear the yoke in their youth, they set themselves up for failure.

Years ago, I met a man in London. He said God had called him to be a pastor. He told me that it was his mission to build a church for God. And I asked him, “What are you waiting for?”

He said, “I am putting things together. I will start soon.” I warned him that he needed to start his church immediately. There were certain difficulties and trials he needed to go through. “The sooner the better,” I told him.

However, he delayed in starting the church. Eventually, he started the church and went straight from one crisis to another. Instead of having a successful ministry, he ended up having one disaster after another. The burden of a new church and the financial realities soon caught up with this middle-aged man who had several children by this time. Indeed, he was completely overwhelmed by the church. The last time I saw him, he was begging for money. Because he did not bear his yoke in time, he turned into a beggar instead of a successful minister.

Never forget this important truth: there is a yoke/difficult part of ministry. It is good that you pass through this stage in your youth. “It is good for a man that he bear the yoke in his youth” (Lamentations 3:27).

Indeed, there are other yokes that you must bear in your youth. Every young man becomes an old man. Every man has sexual desires and needs. When you begin the ministry as a young man, you will encounter sexual desires and temptations. You will be rigorously tested in your youth. When you bear this yoke in your youth, you are likely to do well later on.

Those who mess around with fornication, adultery and other forms of immorality in their youth, grow up to become deceptive, immoral and perverted men. Those who do not overcome this yoke in their youth often become abusers of young ladies in the congregation. As the years go by, these people who do not bear the yoke in their youth are faced with the realities of uncured immorality that they did not resist in their youth. It is important to bear your yoke in your youth!
