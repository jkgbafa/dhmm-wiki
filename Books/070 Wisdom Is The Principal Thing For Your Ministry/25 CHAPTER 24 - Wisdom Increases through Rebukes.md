### CHAPTER 24\

Wisdom Increases through Rebukes\
======================================================

And that from a child thou hast known THE HOLY SCRIPTURES, WHICH ARE ABLE TO MAKE THEE WISE unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus. All SCRIPTURE is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for REPROOF, for correction, for instruction in righteousness:

2 Timothy 3:15-16

The verse above teaches us that the Holy Scriptures make you wise. In the same breath, it teaches us that the Holy Scripture is used for rebukes and correction. This is proof that rebukes from the Lord are necessary to make us wise.

A rebuke is a sharp expression of disapproval and rejection. Rebukes are necessary for correction. People who are never rebuked, turn out badly.

There is a time where gentle counsel, advice, encouragement, warnings, suggestions, propositions, promptings and submission of ideas are helpful. However, there is a time when gentle counsel, advice, encouragement, warnings, suggestions, propositions, promptings and submission of ideas cannot change a person. This is the time where rebukes must come in.

Proud people do not like to be rebuked. A rebuke is sure to send a proud person spinning. Make sure you do not rebuke a proud person. He will only hate you.

Reprove not a scorner, lest he hate thee: rebuke a wise man, and he will love thee.

Proverbs 9:8

God is going to increase your wisdom through rebukes. Throughout your journey with the Lord, expect to be rebuked at one time or the other. Know that wisdom is going to come to you.

One day, I was on holiday with my grandfather in Switzerland, having a jolly time with my grand parents. One evening, my grandfather pointed his walking stick at me and spoke angrily in German. He rebuked me sharply for leaving on the lights in the corridor. I was used to leaving the lights on everywhere. That was a turning point for me. From that time, I received wisdom about how to use electricity and how to save a lot of money by turning off lights. This rebuke happened to me long before electricity became as expensive as it is today. Today, I see people trying hard to learn what I learnt from my grandfather’s rebuke many years ago.

One day I spoke to the famous evangelist, T.L. Osborn. I asked him, “How can I know where to have crusades?” I asked him, “Which town and which city should I go to?” He responded sharply, “Don’t be spooky! Don’t be mystical. Just go wherever there are people who need the Lord. Just choose a city and go there!”

That was a rebuke to set me on my way without excuse.

On another occasion, I was invited to have lunch with the great Archbishop Benson Idahosa and another bishop. This amazing lunch took place in an expensive hotel in the centre of London. In the middle of the meal, I stopped eating because I was more interested in talking to these great men of God than in eating.

Suddenly, the Archbishop Idahosa turned to me and asked whether I had finished eating. I responded that I had finished.

He looked at me quizzically and said, “This is expensive food. Eat everything on your plate.”

I was stunned by this command; but I hurriedly ate up everything on my plate.

I never forgot that rebuke.

It made me appreciate the expensive lunch I had been invited to. I realised that I had taken everything for granted.

Archbishop Idahosa told me that seventy per cent of all pastors in Africa were either his “products” or “products of his products”. I realised even more that I was with a great person and I needed to cherish every moment and every opportunity I was enjoying. I will never forget that lunch. I will never forget the interaction, the counsel and the rebukes I received.

You will find out that sharp rebukes and sharp criticisms of your life will release great bolts of wisdom into your soul. Do not be angry at sharp rebukes. They are great stepping-stones into your wisdom bank.
